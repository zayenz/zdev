## Assessment

Explain the search problem, which mechanisms are usable and what justifies the
current default.

## What runs, and who coordinates it?

Explain the shared problem, search methods, result ownership and coordinator.
Use one concrete example to distinguish actual decisions from derived state.

## How do phases, budgets and stops work?

Separate first solution, improvement and proof. Explain state preserved across
restarts or parking, and distinguish stop from exhaustive completion.

## What makes a useful neighbourhood or partition?

Inspect what is fixed or free in the problem's real decisions. Relate setup
and propagation costs to the freedom left for search.

## What information can methods share safely and usefully?

Explain validity scope, application points and communication cost. Separate
sound restrictions from heuristic guidance.

## What does each mechanism contribute?

Compare equal resource budgets and record trajectories, final quality and
proof outcomes. Separate useful fixed configurations from adaptive policies.

## Which research ideas fit the existing mechanisms?

Use primary sources and explain assumptions. Distinguish implemented behaviour
from possible extensions and an oracle comparison from a runtime policy.

## Findings and next steps

Prioritise demonstrated causes and the experiments that could change defaults.
